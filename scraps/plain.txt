1. Business Overview
Business Name:
Menopause Understood

Mission:
To provide warm, supportive and evidence-based menopause education and peer support for women, delivered in a way that is accessible, flexible and genuinely useful.

The Problem We're Solving:
Menopause Understood exists for the woman moving through peri or post-menopause who feels sad, isolated and dismissed — and has no real idea what is happening to her body or what to do about it. She has tried looking online, but there is either far too much information or not enough of the right kind, and it all becomes overwhelming. So it feels easier to just "suck it up" and get on with the million other things she has to deal with, rather than deal with her hormones. Menopause Understood meets her with warmth, clarity and genuine community, so she doesn't have to work this out alone.

The Shift We Create:
The evidence is consistent: women going through perimenopause and menopause commonly feel dismissed, isolated, confused, overwhelmed, unsupported and frustrated. Menopause Understood exists to shift each of those, deliberately — but not all at once, and not from a single touchpoint. What one course can realistically deliver in an hour is different from what the whole business delivers over months, and being honest about that distinction keeps both the content and the marketing accurate.

Within Each Course — Course-Level Outcomes:
Validated — from dismissed and confused. A single course can make her feel "this is real, and I'm not imagining it."
Clear — from overwhelmed. One well-structured hour can genuinely simplify and organise a single topic.
Reassured and calm — from frustrated. She leaves with a next step and a sense that this is manageable.

Across the Business Over Time — Ecosystem-Level Outcomes:
These need more than one course or session — they build across the whole ecosystem, in this order, mirroring the actual path a woman walks through the business:

1. Informed — from confused. The cumulative, ongoing education promise as she moves through orientation, symptom deep-dives, specialist topics and advocacy content over time. This is the core educational promise of the business — different from "Clear," which is a single moment, not a trajectory.
2. Connected — from isolated. Real, peer-to-peer connection with other women who understand, delivered through the peer groups and workshops.
3. Supported — from unsupported. The business itself being reliably there for her over time — ongoing access, resources and presence, distinct from peer bonding.
4. At ease during her menopause journey — the long-term, cumulative result of the three outcomes above coming together. The north star for the whole ecosystem, not a single course's job.

The Menopause Understood Ecosystem:
The website acts as a central hub where women can engage at whatever level suits them — from a free resource, to a self-paced course, to an ongoing peer support community.

Peer Group — "In This Together"
A 9-week peer support term, capped at 8 women, one live online session per week. Content runs one track per week as a rotating calendar (Track 2, being the largest, is split across two weeks to avoid rushing five symptom domains into one session). Each week flows education and discussion together throughout the hour, rather than a rigid 30/30 split. Course content acts as optional pre-reading; the live session focuses on how topics actually show up in real life and in the room, not a rehash of course material.
Price: $250 for the full 9-week term
Structure: fixed 8-week cohorts plus a 1-week enrollment/rest break between terms (the break period also doubles as the enrollment window for the next cohort)
Capacity: waitlist opens once a cohort is full; spots free up naturally as each term ends
Every course, regardless of track, points to the same evergreen "In This Together" landing page as its closing call to action — the page itself (not the course content) reflects whether the current cohort is open or waitlisted
Community Workshops
Free for the first 2–3 sessions to build early goodwill and profile, then $20 per person once that trial period ends. Workshops are scheduled only during the peer group's break weeks, protecting live-session capacity during active cohort terms — and at least one break period each cycle stays fully protected as genuine rest, rather than every break becoming workshop time by default.
Workplace Education Packages
Three fixed packages rather than fully bespoke content per client — this keeps prep manageable and pricing consistent, while still feeling tailored in delivery and framing.

3. Income Goals
This section sets out two different things, deliberately kept separate: the realistic near-term milestones this plan is actually built to hit, and a long-term north star you're aiming toward with no fixed timeline. Being honest about the gap between the two matters more than making the numbers look tidy.

Near-Term Milestones (What This Plan Is Built to Hit)
These match the staged rollout in Section 10 and reflect what your current hard limits — max 2 live sessions/week, 3–5 hours/week total — can realistically produce.

Stage 3 (Months 5–7): $300–600/month — more tracks built, first free community workshops running

Long-Term Vision: $25,000/Month
The long-term goal is $25,000/month, with no fixed timeline, intended to eventually replace full-time income. This is a genuine north star worth holding onto — but it's important to be honest that it does not fit inside the current operating model. The best-case ceiling built from today's hard limits (Section 9) is roughly $4,120/month, with a realistic established figure closer to $2,700/month (see Section 2). Closing a gap that size is not a matter of selling a bit harder within the same structure; it requires real structural change, made only when capacity and timing actually allow it.

What Would Actually Need to Change to Get There
More hours alone won't get there: the Friday 9am\u20135pm block plus quiet-uni-time roughly doubles available hours (from ~3\u20135 to ~8\u201310/week), which speeds up course-building and workplace outreach \u2014 but the 2-live-sessions-per-week cap and recovery-day rule (Section 9) are unchanged, and that's the real ceiling on peer group, workshop and workplace delivery volume, not raw hours
Workplace pricing is already corrected (Section 2's three packages, priced $100–175/person with real minimums) — this lever is built, not still needed
A higher-ticket flagship offer, since even the full ~25-course catalogue at $27 each has a real ceiling without one
Ways to earn without trading more live hours — e.g. licensing content or training other practitioners/GPs to deliver it
A conscious, future decision to loosen the session/hour caps in Section 9 — not something to do by accident or under pressure

None of this needs deciding now. For as long as sustainability matters more than growth speed (Section 9), the near-term milestones above remain the real plan. $25k/month stays on the horizon as something to revisit deliberately, when and if capacity genuinely changes.

Track 1 Course Outline — "Am I in Perimenopause? Understanding Every Stage of the Journey"
The old 7-module, 60-minute foundational course is superseded by this single 30-minute Track 1 course. It follows the same validate → clarify → reassure template used across the whole catalogue, and deliberately covers every stage of the journey (not just perimenopause symptoms) so a woman isn't misled into ruling herself in or out based on partial information.
Open — Validate (2–3 min): name the sadness, isolation, dismissal and confusion she's likely arriving with
Body — Clarify (20–22 min): walk through every stage — premenopause, early perimenopause, late perimenopause, menopause, postmenopause — what's common at each, in plain English
Close — Reassure + action (5–6 min): one concrete next step (e.g. a simple two-week symptom-tracking habit), a clear note that this is educational orientation and not a diagnosis, and a single soft mention of “In This Together” as where to go deeper with other women

7. Post-Course Feedback — Choosing What's Next
No pre-launch survey. Instead, once a woman finishes the Track 1 course, a short follow-up email goes out asking what she thought and which topic she'd like to see next. This is lighter-weight than a formal survey, and it's real behavioural data from someone who has actually experienced the course — more useful than hypothetical answers gathered before anything existed.

Send timing: 3–5 days after course completion, once the experience is still fresh.

What to ask:
What she liked and didn't like about the course
Which topic from the roadmap (Section 2) she'd want to see built next
Anything else she wishes had been covered

Results directly inform which track gets built next — consistent with the build-order note in Section 2 (decided by real feedback, not fixed in advance).
